# Definition of an Atomic Fact
An Atomic Fact is a complete sentence that:
1. Contains exactly ONE fact (a single claim, finding, statistic, or piece of information).
2. Is semantically independent — fully understandable without any surrounding context.
3. Has all pronouns resolved (replace "it", "they", "this", "these", "the authors", "we", "our team", etc. with their actual referents inferred from context).
4. Expands acronyms on first appearance, e.g., "MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)".
5. Includes necessary qualifiers (e.g., "in this study", "in patients with X", "in mice") so the fact stands alone without the original surrounding text.
6. NEVER introduces information not present in the source text — every detail in the fact must be traceable to the source.

# What NOT to do
- Do not split a fact into multiple sub-facts if splitting destroys meaning or creates incomplete claims.
- Do not paraphrase loosely — stay close to the source's intended meaning, only changing wording to achieve decontextualization.
- Do not include vague claims like "the topic is important" or "more research is needed" — only extract concrete factual content.
- Do not include the source text's structural or meta sentences (e.g., "This paper presents...", "Here we show that..." should be transformed to extract the actual finding, not the meta-statement).
- Do not invent specifics that are not in the source (e.g., do not add a percentage if the source only says "reduced").

}

The "source_sentence" field must be a verbatim substring of the source text (preserve any unusual spacing or punctuation exactly as it appears). The "fact" field is your decontextualized version.
