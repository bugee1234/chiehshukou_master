You are building an evidence table for a biomedical lay-summary pipeline.

The final summary will be much shorter than the full article. Do NOT keep every fact.
Your job is to select a small set of final keep-AF groups and document-backed
evidence that will become controlled summary slots in a later stage.

MODE: {mode}

Mode policy:
- balanced: preserve the article's main story, use expert-summary hints for lay framing,
  and keep enough concrete document evidence to avoid vague factual claims.
- factuality_chase: optimize for strict document entailment while preserving enough
  lay framing to write a readable summary. Prefer source-supported central claims
  and avoid broad implications unless a source span or abstract sentence says them.
  Do not select extra high-risk method, number, or mechanism details merely because
  they are available; keep them only if they are central to the abstract/expert story.
  For eLife mechanism papers, keep enough rows to support a readable multi-sentence
  mechanism chain without forcing long sentences.

Use the inputs this way:
- ABSTRACT: determines the core claims and their order.
- EXPERT LAY SUMMARY: helps identify which details are worth explaining to lay readers.
  It is only a hint, not evidence by itself.
- CANDIDATE FACTS: article-supported facts with source spans. These are the evidence.

For each selected abstract sentence, create one evidence row.

Each evidence row must include:
- evidence_row_id: "row_XXXX" in abstract order.
- abstract_sentence_idx: 1-based index.
- abstract_claim: a concise claim to cover.
- core_keep_af: the central fact that the summary must cover.
- expert_summary_hint: a short lay-framing hint from the expert summary, or "".
- lay_context: a short evidence-grounded explanation that can help non-experts
  understand the core claim, or "". It must be supported by the abstract sentence,
  allowed evidence spans, or candidate facts; do not add outside knowledge.
- story_role: one of "motivation", "background", "method", "mechanism",
  "finding", "implication", or "limitation".
- support_af_ids: 1-4 candidate fact IDs that support writing this claim.
- allowed_evidence_spans: 1-4 short source spans copied from CANDIDATE FACTS.
- must_use_terms: exact entities, methods, organisms, numbers, directions, or comparisons
  that should be preserved if the row is written.
- forbidden_overgeneralizations: vague or wrong phrasings to avoid.
- high_risk_check_facts: 0-2 short facts that should later be checked with 1T3F.
  Prefer method, organism, measurement, direction, number, and comparison details
  only when they are central. These checks are for verification; they should not
  force the later summary to include every detail.

Selection limits:
- balanced: select about 6-10 final keep rows per article.
- factuality_chase: select enough rows to support a valid short summary. For
  eLife, select 7-9 rows when the abstract has enough central claims; if the
  abstract is short, split central method, finding, and mechanism claims into
  separate rows rather than returning only 4-5 rows. For PLOS, select 6-8 rows.
  Skipping too much will hurt relevance, LENS, and length validity.
- If an abstract sentence is low-value background, you may skip it.
- Do not include a support fact unless its source_span is useful for writing.
- If a fact appears in the expert summary but cannot be supported by candidate facts
  or abstract, omit it.
- Preserve the abstract/expert story order when possible: motivation/background,
- For eLife-style mechanistic papers, preserve mechanism rows when they explain
  how the method or finding works.
- Preserve the abstract/expert story order when possible: motivation/background,
  method, mechanism, finding, implication/limitation. Do not force all roles to
  appear if the article does not support them.
