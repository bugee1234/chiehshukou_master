You are selecting candidate atomic facts from a biomedical research article for an abstract-aligned lay summary.

Your task is NOT to extract every article fact. Extract facts that could plausibly appear in a short lay summary whose core scientific claims align with the article abstract: background, main question, key methods at a high level, principal findings, and significance.

Critical policy for this pipeline:
- Missing a core abstract-level claim is the most dangerous error.
- Over-extracting is acceptable when the fact is plausibly useful for an abstract-aligned lay summary.
- Do NOT judge facts against an expert lay summary. Judge only whether the fact is article-supported and summary-worthy.
- Prefer facts that could map to one sentence in the abstract or to one lay-friendly sentence preserving the same core claim.
- Module 2 will later keep only facts that can be entailed from a specific abstract sentence, so include article-supported core claims even if wording differs from the abstract.

# KEEP a fact if it is
- A core finding, conclusion, implication, or take-home message supported by the article.
- Background needed to understand the study question, disease context, biological system, or why the research matters.
- A high-level description of what was done, what was measured, or why a model organism, method, or dataset was used.
- A key mechanism, relationship, comparison, direction of effect, or outcome central to the study story.
- Clinically, translational, diagnostic, therapeutic, or public-health relevance stated in the source.
- Motivation facts: what was unknown, why existing methods are limited, or why the question matters.
- Definitions or bridge facts a lay reader would need to understand the main result.

# Prefer facts from
- Title, abstract block, introduction, author summary, results at a high level, discussion, and conclusion.
- Passages that state WHAT was found or WHY it matters, not HOW a routine step was performed.

# Do NOT extract
- Citation-only, related-work-only, figure/table reference, or intra-paper structural information.
- Routine protocol steps, reagent details, software settings, instrument settings, or statistical minutiae.
- Fine-grained quantitative detail unlikely to affect the summary's main message.
- Operational methods details unless the method itself is the main conclusion.
- Near-duplicate facts when one broader fact already covers the same claim.

# Atomic fact requirements
Each fact must:
1. Contain exactly ONE fact.
2. Be understandable without surrounding context.
3. Resolve pronouns and vague references when possible.
4. Include necessary qualifiers (organism, disease, population, intervention, condition).
5. Never introduce information not present in the source text.

Return ONLY valid JSON:
{
  "candidate_atomic_facts": [
    {
      "fact": "<one decontextualized candidate atomic fact>",
      "source_span": "<verbatim source sentence or short passage supporting this fact>",
      "reasoning": "<one short sentence: why this is summary-worthy / abstract-relevant>"
    }
  ]
}

SOURCE TEXT:
{source_text}
