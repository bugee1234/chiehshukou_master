V9 objective, based on the v7/v8 pilot results:
- First priority: factual correctness and document entailment. Keep the V8
  discipline: every sentence must be supported by one evidence row.
- Second priority: readability as a hard constraint. The v8 outputs had strong
  factuality but weak FKGL/DCRS/CLI because they used dense technical wording.
  Use short sentences, fewer hard words, and aliases after first mention.
- Third priority: relevance to the expert lay summary and abstract. Cover the
  central disease/problem, method/intervention, result, number/comparison, or
  mechanism chain only when directly supported by an evidence row.

Inputs:
- ABSTRACT controls the main story and order.
- EVIDENCE TABLE contains the only document-backed details you may use.
- Each evidence row is a final keep-AF group. Its core_keep_af is the source for
  the claim sentence.

Global rules:
- Write for non-expert readers, but stay evidence-local.
- Do not add outside knowledge.
- Prefer fewer claims that are fully supported over many precise but risky details.
- Do not add a claim only to improve ROUGE/METEOR. Relevance gains are useful
  only when the claim is tightly anchored in core_keep_af or abstract_claim.
- Make every sentence self-contained. AlignScore evaluates each sentence against
  the source document, so avoid sentence subjects like "This", "These", "It",
  "They", "This change", "This finding", or "This means". Name the entity,
  method, condition, or result inside the sentence.
- Preserve exact named entities, organisms, methods, directions, comparisons,
  measurements, and numbers only when they are essential to the central claim.
- Do not use broad implications unless the same evidence row's abstract_claim or
  core_keep_af is itself an implication.
- Avoid unsupported implication/metaphor phrases: "suggests", "may be able",
  "could help", "important for", "acted like", "call for help".
- Do not use metaphors or analogies, even simple ones. They often lower
  sentence-level factual alignment.
- Do not add general definitions, public-health background, or textbook facts
  unless the evidence row itself states them.
- Use causal verbs such as "causes", "ensures", "confirms", "allows", "makes",
  "drives", or "explains" only when the evidence row states that causal relation.
- Use short sentences. One factual relation per sentence.
- Aim for 9-15 words per sentence. A sentence above 18 words is usually too
  long. Split it unless an exact name makes that impossible.
- Avoid dense noun strings. Do not put a method name, organism, gene/protein,
  condition, and result in the same sentence.
- Do not merely copy evidence spans.
- Use aliases actively: keep each essential technical term on first mention,
  then use a short plain alias later when it is unambiguous.
- Do not add paper-like wording such as "transcriptome", "posttranscriptional",
  "intramolecular", "predominantly", or "pathologies" unless the exact word is
  needed for an evidence anchor. Prefer simpler wording when the fact remains
  exact.
- Do not replace exact factual anchors with vague language. Exact anchors matter
  more than readability when they control a claim.

- Each evidence row may contribute at most one claim_sentence and one

- If a clarification would introduce a new claim, leave it empty.
